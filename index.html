Net Run Rate (NRR) is a team's run rate scored minus its run rate conceded across a tournament. Formula: NRR = (total runs scored ÷ total overs faced) − (total runs conceded ÷ total overs bowled). If a team is all out, the full quota of overs is counted.

How Net Run Rate is calculated

NRR = (Total runs scored ÷ Total overs faced) − (Total runs conceded ÷ Total overs bowled)

Step 1: Convert overs to decimals

Cricket overs have 6 balls, so 19.4 overs is 19 + 4/6 = 19.667 overs, not 19.4. Using 19.4 directly is the most common NRR mistake.

Step 2: Apply the all-out rule

If a team is bowled out in 17.2 overs of a 20-over match, NRR counts 20 overs for that innings. This applies both when your team is all out and when your opponent is all out.

Step 3: Add up all matches

NRR uses tournament totals. Add all runs and all overs first, then divide. Do not average each match's NRR.

Frequently asked questions

What is Net Run Rate in cricket?

Net Run Rate (NRR) is a team's run rate scored minus the run rate conceded across a tournament. It is used to separate teams that finish on equal points.

How do you calculate NRR?

NRR = (total runs scored ÷ total overs faced) − (total runs conceded ÷ total overs bowled). Overs such as 19.4 mean 19 overs and 4 balls, which is 19.667 overs.

What happens to NRR if a team is all out?

If a team is bowled out before its overs are complete, NRR counts the full quota of overs for that innings, not the overs actually faced.

Can NRR be negative?

Yes. NRR is negative when a team concedes runs faster than it scores them over the tournament.

Are abandoned matches counted in NRR?

Matches with no result are usually left out of NRR. Follow your tournament's own playing conditions.
